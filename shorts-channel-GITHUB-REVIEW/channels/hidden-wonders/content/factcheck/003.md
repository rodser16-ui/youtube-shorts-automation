# Fact-check 003 — Mars Climate Orbiter (1999)

Script: `channels/hidden-wonders/content/scripts/003-mars-climate-orbiter.json` · checked 23-11-2026

Sources consulted:
- **MIB** = NASA, Mars Climate Orbiter Mishap Investigation Board, Phase I Report, 10 Nov 1999: https://llis.nasa.gov/llis_lib/pdf/1009464main1_0641-mr.pdf. The text was extracted from the PDF and read directly; quotes are below.
- **JPL** = NASA/JPL news release, "Mars Climate Orbiter Team Finds Likely Cause of Loss", 30 Sep 1999: https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/mars-climate-orbiter-team-finds-likely-cause-of-loss/
- **NSSDCA** = NASA NSSDCA catalogue, 1998-073A: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/nmc/spacecraft/display.action?id=1998-073A
- Supporting source (not cited in the JSON): IEEE Spectrum, "Why the Mars probe went off course": https://spectrum.ieee.org/why-the-mars-probe-went-off-course. It names the "spacecraft engineers at Lockheed Martin Astronautics Co., Denver, Colo." as the source of the thruster values.

## Key quotes (MIB)
- Root cause: "the failure to use metric units in the coding of a ground software file, 'Small Forces,' used in trajectory models."
- "The output from the SM_FORCES application code as required by a MSOP Project Software Interface Specification (SIS) was to be in metric units of Newton-seconds (N-s). Instead, the data was reported in English units of pound-seconds (lbf-s)."
- "…underestimated the effect on the spacecraft trajectory by a factor of 4.45…"
- TCM-4 targeted "the first periapse altitude … at a distance of 226km"; post-loss estimates "indicated an initial periapsis … of 57 km"; "The minimum periapse altitude considered survivable by MCO is 80 km."
- "the trajectory modelers assumed the data was provided in metric units per the requirements."
- "MCO either was destroyed in the atmosphere or re-entered heliocentric space."
